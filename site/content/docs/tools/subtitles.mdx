Subtitles looks through a folder of videos, shows you which ones are missing subtitles, and downloads them for you. It does not need an account or an API key, and there is no daily limit.

<Shot id="tools/subtitles/scan" />

## Scanning a folder

1. Open **Tools → Subtitles**.
2. Type a folder path, or click the folder button to browse. Press Enter, or click the scan button.
3. Pick the languages you want. English is selected to start with, and you can choose as many as you like.

Parallax lists every video in the folder, grouped by the folder it is in, with a small badge for each language you picked. A green badge means that language is there. A grey one means it is missing. It also looks in a folder called `Subs` or `Subtitles` next to the video, which is where many releases keep theirs.

Subtitle files that sit next to a video and share its name are matched up automatically. `movie.srt`, `movie.en.srt` and `movie.ar.srt` are all picked up, in the common subtitle formats.

At the top you see how many videos you have, how many have subtitles, and how many are missing.

## Downloading in bulk

The big button, which reads something like **Download 11 missing**, gets one subtitle for each missing language, for every video that needs one. A video is only skipped when every language you picked is already there.

Parallax picks the best match for each video based on its name. The progress shows next to the button, and the work also appears on the [Jobs](/docs/getting-started/how-parallax-works#jobs) page. New files are saved next to the video with the language in the name, like `movie.en.srt`.

## Searching by hand

When the automatic pick is wrong, or nothing was found, hover over a video and click the **search** icon on its row. A window opens with the title and year filled in, which you can change. Pick the right subtitle from the list. There are two places it can look, shown as tabs under **Source**: **Subf2m**, which covers most things, and **YTS-Subs**, which is for movies only and needs your TMDB key (add it under **Settings → Keys & Accounts**; until then the tab is greyed out).

## Removing a subtitle

Hover over a green language badge and click the **×** to delete that subtitle file.

## Subtitles that are out of time

Sometimes a subtitle is fine but appears too early or too late, or slowly drifts away from the speech. Sync fixes this. Parallax listens to the sound of the video and lines the subtitle up with it. It works in any language, because it matches when people are speaking, not what they say.

- The **sync** icon on a row fixes one video.
- **Sync** at the top fixes every video in the folder that has a subtitle, including ones you got somewhere else.
- Subtitles can also be synced automatically as soon as they are downloaded. You can turn that on or off with **Auto-sync downloaded subtitles** under **Settings → Subtitles**.

Two tools can do the syncing, and you pick between them with **Subtitle sync engine** in the same place. **alass** is the default and also copes with extra or missing sections, like a break for adverts. **ffsubsync** is simpler and only fixes a constant offset or a small drift.

## Translating a subtitle

Sometimes a video has an English subtitle but not one in the language you want. Parallax can translate it for you, on your own computer. Nothing is sent anywhere, and you do not need an account or a key.

1. Open **Settings → Subtitles** and, under **Language packs**, click **Install** next to the language you want. Arabic, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Russian and Chinese are available. Each is a one-time download of about 140 to 460 MB (Arabic is the largest).
2. Make sure that language is one of your **Default subtitle languages** in the same tab.
3. On the Subtitles page, hover over a video that has an English subtitle and is missing that language. A **translate** icon appears on its row. Click it.

The job shows on the [Jobs](/docs/getting-started/how-parallax-works#jobs) page, where you can also cancel it. A cancelled or failed translation leaves nothing behind. A feature film takes a couple of minutes on a normal processor.

The result is saved next to the video as a machine-made subtitle, for example `movie.ar.mt.srt`. The `mt` in the name marks it as machine-made, so Parallax still counts that language as missing and a real subtitle can still be downloaded later. On the page, the language shows an amber **machine-made** badge instead of a green one, with an **×** to delete it, and in the player's captions menu it reads, for example, "Arabic (machine-made)". When a real subtitle for that language is downloaded, it replaces the machine-made file. Only English subtitles in `.srt` format can be translated, and a real subtitle in that language is never replaced.

Subtitle names can carry two tags after the language, in any order: `hi` for subtitles for the hard of hearing (`movie.ar.hi.srt`) and `mt` for machine-made. A hearing-impaired subtitle is a real subtitle, so it counts as present, and the player labels it "(hearing impaired)".

Sound notes such as `[DISTANT EXPLOSION]`, speaker names, and song lines marked with `♪` are kept: the notes and names are translated, and the `♪` marks are put back around the translated words. You can leave the notes and names out with **Leave out sound notes and speaker names when translating** under **Settings → Subtitles**.

How good a translation is depends on the language. Arabic was checked against real human subtitles for seven films. The other languages were checked against the human subtitles of the short film Sintel and read sensibly: Spanish, Italian and French came closest to the human version, German and Portuguese a little further, and Russian is the loosest, partly because the human Russian subtitles take liberties. Chinese was only checked by reading it. Portuguese leans towards European Portuguese ("comboio" rather than "trem"), and Italian output writes an apostrophe where a native subtitle has an accent ("andra'" for "andrà").

A translation by a machine is good enough to follow the story but it is not perfect. Names and jokes are where it slips most.

## Generating a subtitle from the audio

A brand-new release often has no subtitle anywhere yet. Parallax can listen to the English speech and write a rough subtitle, on your own computer, to use until a real one appears. Nothing is sent anywhere.

1. Open **Settings → Subtitles** and, under **Speech-to-text models**, click **Install** next to **Whisper small** (about 460 MB, a one-time download). **Whisper medium** is bigger and slower but gets names right more often.
2. On the Subtitles page, hover over a video that has no English subtitle. A **microphone** icon appears on its row. Click it.

The job shows on the [Jobs](/docs/getting-started/how-parallax-works#jobs) page and can be cancelled. A cancelled or failed run leaves nothing behind. On a normal processor, Whisper small works through an hour of audio in about six minutes.

The result is saved as a machine-made subtitle, `movie.en.mt.srt`, so it is marked like a [translation](#translating-a-subtitle): the page shows the amber **machine-made** badge, and the real English subtitle is still downloaded when you ask for it and replaces this one. If you also have a language pack installed for one of your other languages, the new subtitle is translated into it straight away.

Expect mistakes. Whisper small hears words well but often writes names wrongly (for example "I would start" for "Howard Stark"). It only understands English speech for now.

## Watching with subtitles

Click the **play** icon on a row to preview the video with its subtitles. Anywhere you play a video in Parallax, a captions button appears when it has subtitle files, and you can choose which language to show.
