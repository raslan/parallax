Compress turns your files into smaller copies. For video you can shrink them while keeping the quality about the same, bring them down to a lower resolution, or both. For music you can pick a smaller format. A big library often gets a lot smaller with no difference you can see or hear.

It is on **Videos → Compress** and **Audio → Compress**.

<Shot id="features/compress/select" />

## The short version

1. Open **Compress** and pick a library.
2. Choose how you want the files to come out in **Encoding settings**.
3. Select the files to compress.
4. Click **Compress** in the bar that appears at the bottom of the page once something is selected.

The page shows a live estimate of how much space you will save before you start, so you can try different settings first without changing anything.

## Encoding settings

Click the bar that says **Encoding settings** to open the settings drawer. The page stays usable while it is open, and the estimates update as you change things.

**Target codec** is the video format of the new files.

- **H.264** plays almost everywhere. It makes moderately smaller files.
- **HEVC (H.265)** makes files roughly 40 to 50 percent smaller than H.264 at the same quality. Most modern phones, TVs and apps play it. This is the default.
- **AV1** can be 50 to 60 percent smaller than H.264, but it takes longer to make and only newer devices and apps play it.

**Encoding speed** lets you trade time for size. Slow takes longer and gives smaller files, and Fast is the other way around.

**Quality** is a slider with a number and a label next to it, from "Visually lossless" through "High quality" and "Good quality" to "Noticeable loss" and "Severe degradation". A lower number keeps more quality and makes a bigger file. A higher number makes a smaller file and loses more detail. The default is a good starting point, and the estimate tells you what a different choice would save.

**Max resolution** is optional. If you pick 1080p, for example, any video bigger than that is scaled down to fit, and videos already at or below it are left alone. This looks at the shorter side of the picture, so tall phone videos are handled correctly too. Leave it on **Original** if you only want to reduce file size.

</Tab>

**Target codec** is the format of the new files: **Opus**, **AAC** or **MP3**. Opus gives the best quality for the size, AAC is widely supported, and MP3 plays on practically anything.

**Bitrate** is how much data each second of sound uses. Higher sounds better and makes a bigger file. The slider has a label next to the number that tells you what to expect, from low-quality voice settings up to "transparent", where you should not be able to hear a difference from the original, and on to archival quality.

A file that is already at or below the bitrate you pick is skipped, so a file is never re-encoded to a higher bitrate than it already had. Tags such as artist and album are kept.

## The estimates

The boxes at the bottom of the drawer show the size of the library, the size of what you have selected, what it should come to afterwards, and the saving. These are estimates, and can be out by around 20 percent for video and 5 percent for audio. If it says your files would grow, move the quality or bitrate toward the smaller end.

## Choosing files

Select files by ticking them, dragging a box around them, or using **All** and **None**. There is also a search box.

On video, cards labelled **Select all that are** pick a group for you and show how many files it covers and what the saving would be:

- **not HEVC** (or whichever format you chose) selects videos that are not already in that format.
- **above 1080p** (or whichever size you chose) shows up once you set a max resolution and selects the videos that are bigger than it.
- **over 1 GB** selects the big files.

Every video card also shows the saving it would get.

## Keeping your originals

**Keep originals** is a switch in the strip at the top of the page, next to the library picker, and it is on by default. When it is on, the old version of each file is moved to an `_originals` folder next to it and the new, smaller one takes its place. You can look through, restore or delete them on the [Originals](/docs/features/originals) page.

If you switch it off, the original is replaced and you cannot get it back. Leave it on until you are sure you like the result.

The new file keeps the old one's dates and permissions, so a media server or photo manager that watches the folder does not treat it as a brand new file.

## While it runs

Compressing happens in the background. A banner on the page shows which file Parallax is working on and how far along it is, and you can cancel at any time. It also appears on the [Jobs](/docs/getting-started/how-parallax-works#jobs) page. The space you save is added to your totals on the Home page.

If you have given Docker a graphics card, Parallax uses it to compress video faster. See [Hardware acceleration](/docs/advanced/hardware-acceleration) if you want to set that up.

To compress several files at the same time, change **Concurrent video transcodes (per GPU)** under **Settings → Transcoder**. Music has its own **Concurrent audio transcodes** setting on the same tab, because it always runs on the processor. These control how many files one job handles at once. **Concurrent jobs** in **Settings → General** is a different setting, for how many whole jobs run side by side.
